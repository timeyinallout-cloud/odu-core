# Getting a second source

Everything verified in this project rests on one book: Bascom (1969). He is
careful and shows his working — he tells you he consulted 86 lists and that 21
other orderings exist — but a single source is a single point of failure for a
project whose entire claim is provenance.

Abimbola is the natural counterweight. He was himself a babaláwo, which Bascom
was not, so he is not merely a second observer but a different *kind* of
witness.

## Status of the two candidates

**Ifá: An Exposition of Ifá Literary Corpus (1976).** Not on the Internet
Archive in any form. Needs a library.

**Sixteen Great Poems of Ifá (1975).** Catalogued in UNESDOC at
`ark:/48223/pf0000019827`, 468 pp. UNESCO does **not** serve the full text and
directs enquiries to its library and archives. Widely miscited as a UNESCO
publication — the imprint is *Niamey: OAU Centre for Linguistic and Historical
Studies by Oral Tradition (CELHTO), 1975*, published with UNESCO's financial
assistance. An unauthorised scan exists on the Internet Archive; this project
does not use it.

## Draft enquiry

To `library@unesco.org`, copying `archives@unesco.org`.

> Subject: Access enquiry — Sixteen Great Poems of Ifá (1975), ark:/48223/pf0000019827
>
> Dear UNESCO Library,
>
> I am building an open, sourced dataset of the 256 Odù Ifá and their
> correspondence to binary values, with Yorùbá orthography preserved. The
> figures are currently verified against Bascom's Ifa Divination (1969), and I
> am seeking a second independent source so the dataset does not rest on a
> single work.
>
> Your catalogue holds Wande Abimbọla's Sixteen Great Poems of Ifá,
> ark:/48223/pf0000019827, archives call number COM.COL.OR/AF.7, published in
> Niamey by the OAU Centre for Linguistic and Historical Studies by Oral
> Tradition with UNESCO's financial assistance. The record notes that full text
> is not available and directs enquiries to you.
>
> Could you advise whether a consultable copy exists — in the UNESCO Library,
> through interlibrary loan, or via CELHTO — and what the rights position is?
> To be clear about intent: I am seeking to verify a table of figures and to
> cite page references. I am not seeking to reproduce the verses, and the
> dataset deliberately stores citations rather than text.
>
> With thanks,
> [name]

## Other routes

- **CELHTO directly** — the actual publisher, now part of the African Union,
  based in Niamey.
- **Library call number 896(662.1) ABI** — WorldCat will find holdings.
- **Any university with an African studies collection.** Bascom 1969 was
  borrowable through the Internet Archive; Abimbola may be too, in time.

## What a second source would settle

1. The 16 figures, independently — the whole foundation.
2. Whether the southwestern ordering used here is confirmed or contested.
3. Compound names beyond the 54 sourced from Bascom.
4. Whether elision shifts the tone of remaining syllables, which is currently
   an open question in `build_bascom_content.py`.
